## The brief

Give an unnamed series a physical world that works for the lens, the actors, and the shooting day. The design had to carry character without crowding the frame. Every practical surface also needed to support lighting, camera movement, and quick company moves.

## Built for camera

Plans were developed around real lens positions rather than a perfect architectural view. Wild walls, removable sections, and concealed access were established before fabrication. That let the crew change a shot without asking the scenery to do something it was never built to do.

Material choices favored convincing texture over unnecessary weight. Carved foam, routed sheet goods, welded frames, and layered scenic paint produced depth where the camera would find it. Off-axis surfaces stayed simpler.

## Ready for the day

The set was labeled, documented, and prepared for resets. Touch-up kits matched the scenic recipes used in the shop. When production moved in, the environment was ready to work: quiet underfoot, accessible behind the walls, and finished wherever the lens could reach.
